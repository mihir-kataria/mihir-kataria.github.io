AI & Documentation

Writing for Two Readers: People and the Retriever

In a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system, your documentation becomes the AI's source of truth: the retriever pulls chunks of it, and the model answers from them. When I evaluated AIAG's answers with DeepEval and Ragas, low faithfulness and context precision scores often traced back to the content, not the model: vague headings, answers buried mid-page, and one concept called three different names.

The fix isn't writing for machines instead of people. Descriptive headings, self-contained sections, and one term per concept make docs easier to retrieve and easier to read. This piece shows how, and why technical writers now shape an AI product's accuracy, not just its manual.

By Mihir Kataria · 8 min read Related sample: How AIAG Answers a Question ↗

Docs-as-Code & AI

Docs-as-Code in the Age of AI: Draft Fast, Verify Everything

I rebuilt AIAG's documentation the way engineers ship software: Markdown in Git, diagrams written as Mermaid code, and every change reviewed as a diff before it went live on GitHub Pages. An AI assistant drafted faster than I ever could, but every claim still had to be checked against the codebase, and that verification step is where a technical writer earns their place.

Seventy Freshmen, Six Lectures: What Teaching Taught Me About Writing for Beginners

In front of 70 first-year students, I learned that a confused audience rarely asks questions. It just stops following. The habits that fixed my lectures (lead with the goal, one idea per step, define each term the first time it appears, and show what success looks like) are the same ones that make documentation work for a first-time user.
